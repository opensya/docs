You find out how much control you have over your software when something essential has to change. An organization should be able to switch providers, hand a project over to another team, or launch a new service. It should be able to do so while protecting its data, preserving the work already done, and keeping the services its users rely on running.

That is the freedom we want to preserve. We build open software building blocks so teams can build on the choices they make today and remain free to revisit them tomorrow.

## Software shapes our world

Requesting time off, paying a bill, and accessing a service are ordinary tasks. Behind each one, software determines what people can do, how long it takes, and what happens when something falls outside the usual process.

For an organization, staying in control of that software means being able to improve the service it provides as users’ needs change. The choices we make today help determine what services we can provide tomorrow.

## Building should leave us free to choose

Choosing a tool means accepting trade-offs. A service that already fits into a system can speed up a launch. When the time comes to renew a contract or switch providers, a business should be able to assess what that decision means for its data, its budget, and its ability to keep operating.

Understanding those consequences early gives teams time to decide. They can keep a tool that works for them, plan a transition, or change part of their software while preserving the work already done.

## What we believe

**We believe that the teams who know their users best should be able to decide how their tools evolve.**

Developers should be able to choose a solution that fits the problem at hand. Organizations should be able to decide where their data is hosted, who manages it, and how to retrieve it if their needs change.

That freedom gives lasting value to the work already done. It lets teams build on an existing application to meet a new need and invest in a project knowing it can evolve with their operations.

## Sovereignty includes the ability to build

**Opensya began in Senegal.** We want teams building here to draw on technology from around the world and, in turn, create tools that others can use, adapt, and improve.

For us, digital sovereignty takes shape through practical decisions. An organization can choose how it handles its data. A team can develop the skills it needs to maintain a tool it relies on. A community can contribute to technology that meets its needs.

We want this work to be useful in Senegal and wherever teams face the same problems. We believe every region of the world should be able to take part in creating the technologies we all depend on.

## Why Opensya exists

Many teams spend time solving the same basic problems. A business launching a second service, for example, has to decide how customers will access it. With a building block designed for use across applications, the team can work toward a simpler customer experience and spend more time on what the new service delivers.

We build open, independent, reusable software building blocks to make that kind of choice possible. Each one addresses a specific need. A team should be able to adopt it on its own in an existing project and combine it with its own tools or other building blocks when the project calls for it.

## Openness has to work in practice

Before adopting a tool, a team should be able to understand what it offers, what it takes to integrate, and what it will need to maintain it. An open license gives the team rights to the code. Clear documentation and examples help it decide how to use it.

Making openness useful is a responsibility we take on for the long term. A team that comes across a case we did not anticipate should know how to report it, propose a fix, and understand the decisions that follow. That is how a need discovered in one project can benefit others.

## Building to last

A building block should remain useful as the project changes or another team takes over maintenance. Readable code and documented decisions help that team understand what it has inherited and move forward with confidence.

Longevity depends on everyday changes, too. A fix should be easy to understand. An update should explain what it changes for teams already using the building block. We want to give this work the time it deserves. That is how a building block can keep serving people long after its launch.

## Independence needs lasting support

An organization that relies on a building block should be able to assess the resources devoted to maintaining it. We need to fund the time it takes to fix problems, improve documentation, and support changes over time.

We want to build a business model that sustains this work. Commercial activities, contributions, partnerships, and investment could all play a part. We will judge each option by its ability to support maintenance and preserve teams’ freedom to use, adapt, and develop the building blocks they depend on.

## Trust is earned through action

A team considering a building block should be able to find out what it can try today, under what conditions, and with what limitations. Anyone considering supporting Opensya should be able to see how far the work has come, what still needs resources, and why priorities sometimes change.

We want to report on our work as our projects progress. We will share what works, what still needs to be verified, and what we have to rethink as people use it. Some ideas will evolve. Some projects will take longer than expected. We owe it to those who trust us to explain these decisions and their consequences.

## An ambition beyond our own projects

A team reuses a building block in another field. A business improves a service while preserving its data and the way its people work. A community improves a tool to meet a need we had never encountered. Each of these uses gives meaning to the work we do at Opensya.

We will know our foundations are useful when others can build on them, improve them, and use them for projects we could not have imagined. Some of those projects will come to life without us ever knowing about them.

**That would be a success.**

**Opensya exists so that those who build today remain free to decide tomorrow what their work can become.**
